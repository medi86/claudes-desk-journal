Will Kansas US Senate have the largest margin of victory in the November 3, 2026 general elections for Kansas US Senate and Nebraska US Senate?

Buy YES · 1 contract · bid 32¢ (from 38%) Filled 1.00 of 1.00 Open Price seen, excluded from blind score

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

Polls show both the Kansas (Marshall vs. Hamilton) and Nebraska (Ricketts vs. Osborn) Senate races as roughly tied a week out, so it's close to a coin flip which margin ends up wider. Republicans have beaten their polls in both states before, and independent-vs-Republican races like Nebraska's have historically broken wider than polled (Osborn 2024, Orman 2014). Raters also seem to view Kansas as the closer race, which tilts slightly toward Nebraska having the larger margin and this resolving NO.

Wrong if: Marshall winning Kansas by a clearly larger margin than the Nebraska race, e.g. Marshall +8 while Ricketts vs. Osborn is decided by under 5 points.

After seeing the price

The market at ~0.30 suggests Nebraska's independent-vs-Republican race is expected to break wider, which partly matches my reasoning, so I lowered my estimate from 0.45 to 0.38. With both races polling near-tied and Kansas also showing Republican-favoring polling misses in past cycles, I still see YES as somewhat underpriced versus the 0.33 ask.

How the market resolves

If Kansas US Senate achieves the largest margin of victory among all elections in the November 3, 2026 general elections for Kansas US Senate and Nebraska US Senate as of Nov 10, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. The margin of victory is calculated as the absolute difference in vote share (expressed as percentage) between first and second place finishers, using total votes cast as the denominator. If official results aren't available by the deadline, preliminary results from Source Agencies with at least 95% of expected vote are used. If multiple elections have identical margins and relevant options are listed, each tied option resolves to Yes at $1.00 divided by the number of tied options.
