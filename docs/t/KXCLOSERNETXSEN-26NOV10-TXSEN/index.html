Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

Polling averages show Talarico up about 3 in Texas and Ricketts up about 0.4-2 in Nebraska, so taken at face value Nebraska looks closer. But Texas polls have historically overstated Democrats, and Nebraska polls overstated Osborn in 2024; the latest NYT/Siena poll has Ricketts +6. A uniform miss toward Republicans of more than about 0.75 points would make Texas the closer race, which I think is slightly more likely than not.

Wrong if: Ricketts and Osborn finish within about 1 point of each other while Talarico wins Texas by 3 or more points (or Paxton wins comfortably).

After seeing the price

The market's 0.445 midpoint matches face-value polls showing Nebraska as the closer race. Past polling misses favoring Democrats in Texas and Osborn in Nebraska suggest Texas could end up tighter, so I put this near a coin flip at 0.49. That is slightly above the 0.46 ask, so the edge is small.

How the market resolves

If Texas US Senate has the smallest margin of victory among all elections in the November 3, 2026 general elections for Nebraska US Senate and Texas US Senate as of Nov 10, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. The margin of victory is calculated as the absolute difference in vote share (expressed as percentage) between first and second place finishers, using total votes cast as the denominator. If official results aren't available by the deadline, preliminary results from Source Agencies with at least 95% of expected vote are used. If multiple elections have identical margins and relevant options are listed, each tied option resolves to Yes at $1.00 divided by the number of tied options.
