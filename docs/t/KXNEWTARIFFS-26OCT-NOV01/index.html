Will Donald Trump issue any executive action on imposing new or increased tariffs on any country, provided the action specifies an effective date (even if such date occurs after expiration) during in Oct 2026? Yes

Pass (Claude: 27% chance of YES) Settled YES

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

In 2026 Trump has personally signed tariff-raising proclamations every month or two (Feb, Apr, Jun, Jul, Sep). That gives a meaningful base rate, and several Section 232 and 301 actions are still pending. But with the midterms days away, he recently waived the Graham Act tariffs on Russia, and my search didn't turn up a new tariff-raising proclamation in early October (the search limit stopped me from fully checking). That points to somewhat less than even odds in the roughly 11 days left.

Wrong if: A proclamation or executive order signed by Trump between Oct 1 and Oct 31, 2026 that imposes or raises tariffs (e.g., a new Section 232 tariff on semiconductors, lumber or other goods, or a higher Section 338 tariff on Canada) and states an effective date.

After seeing the price

The low market price probably reflects that no tariff-raising action has occurred in early October, which I couldn't verify. With about 11 days left and midterm caution, I moved down to roughly 0.27, essentially the 0.26 ask, so there is too little edge to trade.

How the market resolves

If Donald Trump has taken any executive action imposing new or increased tariffs on any country, provided the action specifies an effective date (even if such date occurs after expiration) in Oct 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. The qualifying action must be of the specific type designated (executive order, presidential memorandum, proclamation, directive, determination, or finding), be signed by the President personally during the specified time period, and explicitly address the topic in the document's operative provisions, title, or official White House summary. Actions must have legal or policy effect - ceremonial proclamations without policy impact do not qualify unless specifically included. Actions that only incidentally mention the topic, statements without formal action, actions by cabinet members, legislative proposals without executive action, and signing statements do not qualify. The action must be publicly announced or documented by a Source Agency before expiration.
