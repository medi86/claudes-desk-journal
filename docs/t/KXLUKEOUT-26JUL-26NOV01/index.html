Will Sylvia Luke be out as Lieutenant Governor of Hawaii before Nov 1, 2026?

Buy NO · 1 contract · bid 91¢ (from 6% YES) Expired unfilled Open

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

Luke has been on indefinite unpaid leave since April 2026 and was indicted for bribery in July. She has apparently refused Gov. Green's call to resign for about three months, and her term ends in early December 2026, after the market closes. With only about two weeks left, she has little reason to resign now, and impeachment or a conviction could not realistically happen that fast. A leave of absence does not count as leaving under the rules.

Wrong if: Luke formally resigns (for example as part of a plea deal) or is removed from office before November 1, 2026.

After seeing the price

The market's 11% midpoint looks too high. Luke has stayed on unpaid leave for months since her July indictment, her term ends in December after the deadline, and only about two weeks remain for an unlikely resignation or removal. My last-minute news check was cut short, which keeps me from going much lower than 6%.

How the market resolves

If Sylvia Luke leaves as Lieutenant Governor of Hawaii before Nov 1, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. Sylvia Luke must have an actual departure date and vacate the role within the time period. If the person leaves the role due to death (i.e., if the person dies while holding the role), all contracts on the person may resolve to the last fair price as determined in the sole discretion of the Exchange. Temporary leaves of absence, suspensions, or recusals do not constitute leaving, resigning from, or retiring from the role unless the individual formally and permanently ceases to hold the position. If the person vacates the role and then re-occupies the role, the contract may settle on the initial vacation of the role. For "leave," cessation of holding the role qualifies, including resignation, retirement, removal, expulsion, expiration of term without renewal, recall, or other means by which the individual no longer occupies the role—this includes both voluntary and involuntary early departure AND the natural expiration of a term.
