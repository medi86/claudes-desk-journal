Will HHS's transgender Medicaid rule take effect Oct. 13? Yes

Pass (Claude: 78% chance of YES) Settled YES

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

CMS finalized CMS-2451-F on Aug 11, 2026, published it Aug 13, and set an Oct. 13 effective date. California's Medicaid agency has already issued implementation guidance. The rule takes effect automatically unless a court issues a nationwide stay or vacatur before then. That is a real possibility given the legal challenges likely to come and the 2025 pattern of courts pausing HHS rules under the APA, but a ruling limited to the plaintiff states would still mean YES.

Wrong if: A federal court issues a universal APA Section 705 stay, vacatur, or nationwide injunction of CMS-2451-F before October 13, 2026, so the rule cannot operate in any jurisdiction that day.

After seeing the price

My earlier 0.70 came from limited information; I couldn't find out whether a lawsuit or nationwide stay was pending, and the market may know litigation risk is low. The gap to 0.835 is too small for a confident NO bet.

How the market resolves

If the taking effect of CMS-2451-F in at least one U.S. jurisdiction on October 13, 2026 is confirmed by any of the Source Agencies after Issuance and before Oct 20, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. This must be confirmed, verified, or established as factual by at least one Source Agency. News confirmation is defined as a published article, broadcast segment, or official news report by a Source Agency, that A) explicitly states the taking effect of CMS-2451-F in at least one U.S. jurisdiction on October 13, 2026 occurred, is true, or has been verified, B) based on journalistic investigation, sources, or evidence, and C) presented as factual reporting rather than speculation, rumor, or unverified allegation. For this market, taking effect means CMS-2451-F operates in at least one U.S. jurisdiction on Oct. 13. Announcement, publication, or scheduling alone does not qualify. Nationwide relief preventing operation anywhere in the United States that day means No; limited relief does not if the rule operates elsewhere. The existing hormone-therapy taper and continued mental-health funding do not negate legal effect.
