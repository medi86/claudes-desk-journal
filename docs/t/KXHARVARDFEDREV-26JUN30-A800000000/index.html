Will Harvard's federal sponsored revenue be above 800000000 in fiscal year 2026? Above 800 million

Buy NO · 1 contract · bid 57¢ (from 18% YES) Resting Open

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

Harvard's federal sponsored revenue was $629M in FY2025 and about $684M in FY2024, and even the no-freeze path pointed to only about $745M. Passing $800M in FY2026 would take a jump of about 27%, while the administration is still hostile and new awards are likely slower. The main way to YES is a large one-time catch-up reimbursement for freeze-period costs being booked as FY2026 federal revenue.

Wrong if: Harvard's FY2026 Annual Financial Report (around October 2026) shows federal sponsored revenue above $800M, for example because catch-up reimbursements for frozen grants were recognized in FY2026.

After seeing the price

The market's 45% made me allow more weight for a one-time catch-up reimbursement being booked as FY2026 federal revenue, so I raised my estimate from 6% to 18%. Even so, $800M would be a record by a wide margin and needs a 27% jump over FY2025, so I still see the market as too high.

How the market resolves

If Harvard's federal sponsored revenue is above 800 million dollars in fiscal year 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. The figure is the federal sponsored revenue stated in the Financial Overview section of Harvard University's Annual Financial Report for fiscal year 2026, the year ending June 30, 2026. If that section states no federal figure, the sum of the federal direct and indirect cost lines under Sponsored support in the same report's Consolidated Statements of Changes in Net Assets governs. The following will NOT be used to resolve the market: the expenditure total in the report's sponsored support note, which also counts federal work subcontracted to Harvard by other institutions, and any Fact Book total. The figure as first published governs, and a later restatement or reclassification is disregarded.
