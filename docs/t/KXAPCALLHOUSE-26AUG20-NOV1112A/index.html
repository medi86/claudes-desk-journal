Will AP call the 2026 U.S. House majority before 12AM ET on Nov 11, 2026? Before Nov 11

Pass (Claude: 81% chance of YES) Open Price seen, excluded from blind score

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

Polls before the Nov. 3, 2026 election showed Democrats about 8-9 points ahead on the generic ballot, and forecasts projected a large Democratic House majority. In past wave years like 2018, AP called the House on election night. Only very close years like 2022 and 2024, when slow Western counts decided control, pushed the call past a week. The window here runs about a week after the election, so a call is likely unless the race is much closer than polls suggested.

Wrong if: Republicans beat their polls enough that control comes down to a handful of uncalled California/Western seats, and AP has not called the majority by 11:59 PM ET on Nov 10, 2026.

After seeing the price

The market's 0.835 midpoint sits slightly above my estimate. Buying NO at about 0.19 matches my own 0.19 chance of NO, so there is no edge. The market may also reflect late information I couldn't verify, which is reason not to bet against it.

How the market resolves

If the Associated Press makes its first definitive call of a winner for the 2026 U.S. House majority before 12AM ET on Nov 11, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. The call must be an explicit declaration that a candidate, party, or option has won, will win, is projected to win, or equivalent clear language indicating the outlet has determined the winner. The call must appear on one of the outlet's primary platforms (main website, television broadcast, or official social media if that is their primary medium) and not be prefaced with qualifiers suggesting uncertainty such as "appears to," "likely to," "probably," or "may have." The relevant time is when the call first becomes publicly visible on the outlet's platform, not when it may have been written, decided internally, or prepared for publication. If the call is later retracted, the market nonetheless resolves to Yes based on the initial call time. Preliminary exit polls, reporting of other outlets' calls, conditional statements, opinion pieces, and technical glitches do not qualify as definitive calls.
