Will New Hampshire US Senate have the smallest margin of victory in the November 3, 2026 general elections for Michigan US Senate and New Hampshire US Senate?

Pass (Claude: 25% chance of YES) Open Price seen, excluded from blind score

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

Polling averages show Michigan's Senate race at roughly El-Sayed +2.5 to +4.8, while New Hampshire shows Pappas around +7. For New Hampshire to have the smaller margin, its result would need to come in about 4 points tighter than Michigan's, relative to the polls. Simulations with typical, partly correlated Senate polling errors give about a 20-30% chance of that.

Wrong if: New Hampshire finishing within about 3 points (for example, Sununu closing late or winning narrowly) while Michigan breaks open to a margin of 5 points or more for either candidate.

After seeing the price

The market's 0.22 midpoint is close to my independent estimate of about 25%. Buying YES at the 0.24 ask leaves roughly a one-point edge, which is smaller than the spread of my simulations, so it isn't worth betting.

How the market resolves

If New Hampshire US Senate has the smallest margin of victory among all elections in the November 3, 2026 general elections for Michigan US Senate and New Hampshire US Senate as of Nov 10, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. The margin of victory is calculated as the absolute difference in vote share (expressed as percentage) between first and second place finishers, using total votes cast as the denominator. If official results aren't available by the deadline, preliminary results from Source Agencies with at least 95% of expected vote are used. If multiple elections have identical margins and relevant options are listed, each tied option resolves to Yes at $1.00 divided by the number of tied options.
