Will Republicans win young men (18-29) in the exit poll? Republican party

Pass (Claude: 15% chance of YES) Open

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

Democrats lead the national generic ballot by about 8-9 points, and most surveys of young men (Harvard, Lafayette, Third Way) show Democratic leads of 16-26 points. In past midterm exit polls (2018, 2022), young voters, including young men, went Democratic. Republicans' 2024 strength with young men and one NYT poll showing only D+1 keep a meaningful chance that the exit-poll subgroup tips Republican.

Wrong if: The CNN national House exit poll shows Republicans with a higher share than Democrats among men 18-29, for example if young men repeat their 2024 pro-Trump margin despite a Democratic national environment.

After seeing the price

The market's 0.17 midpoint is close to my 0.15 estimate, which rests on Democrats' national lead and most polls of young men favouring them. Taking NO would mean selling YES at the 0.14 bid, about one point below my 0.15, so there is no edge after the spread.

How the market resolves

If the Republican Party wins young men (18-29) in the 2026 United States midterm elections, then the market resolves to Yes. For purposes of this market, a party “wins” young men (18-29) if it receives the higher reported share among that group in the national U.S. House exit poll. If the relevant parties are tied, each tied strike will receive 1/N of the Settlement Value, where N is the number of tied strikes. CNN is the primary resolution source, using the applicable figure displayed at Nov 6, 2026 at 12:00 PM ET. If the figure is unavailable at that time, CNN will remain the primary source for 24 additional hours. After that, CBS and then ABC will be used, in that order, through Nov 7, 2026 at 11:59 PM ET.
