Will the highest single-day number of transit calls through the Strait of Hormuz be more than 4 from Oct 5, 2026 to Oct 11, 2026? Above 4

Pass (Claude: 82% chance of YES) Open Price seen, excluded from blind score

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

Daily IMF PortWatch transit counts for Hormuz have been 0-8 recently, and the weekly maximum exceeded 4 in 4 of the last 5 weeks, though the most recent week topped out at exactly 4. An independent tracker (hormuz.now) counted 17 crossings on Oct 5, its highest in a month, and on comparable busy days PortWatch has typically recorded 4-8. A surge in tanker attacks is pushing traffic down and keeps meaningful NO risk.

Wrong if: PortWatch publishes daily counts for Oct 5-11 that all come in at 4 or below, e.g. if attacks deter traffic or PortWatch's AIS-based count stays well below other trackers on Oct 5.

After seeing the price

On a closer look, hormuz.now's count of 17 for Oct 5 maps to more than 4 on PortWatch even at the low end of recent ratios, so I raised my estimate toward the market. The remaining gap of about 3 points against the 0.85 bid is too small to bet NO, given the uncertainty in translating proxy counts into PortWatch numbers.

How the market resolves

If the highest single-day number of transit calls through the Strait of Hormuz as reported by the IMF PortWatch is above 4 from Oct 5, 2026 to Oct 11, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. The Expiration Value is determined by identifying the highest single-day count from Oct 5, 2026 to Oct 11, 2026. The market will resolve no earlier than Tuesday at 9 AM following the end of the period, once complete data is available.
