Will Republicans win independents in the exit poll? Republican party

Buy NO · 1 contract · bid 86¢ (from 6% YES) Filled 1.00 of 1.00 Open

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

In midterms, independents almost always vote against the president's party in exit polls, as in 2006, 2010, 2014 and 2018. In 2026 Trump's approval is about 37%, Democrats lead the generic ballot by 7-8 points, and Marist has Democrats ahead among independents 52-39. For Republicans to win independents, the polls would have to be off by a historically large margin.

Wrong if: CNN's national House exit poll showing Republicans with a higher share of independents than Democrats, for example a late Republican surge or a big polling miss that leaves the national House vote roughly even or Republican.

After seeing the price

The market's 16.5% midpoint is well above my estimate. Democrats lead independents by double digits in recent polling and lead the generic ballot by 7-8 points. In midterms, the out-party nearly always wins independents in the exit poll. I nudged my estimate up slightly to allow for exit-poll noise, but a NO bet still looks clearly favorable.

How the market resolves

If the Republican Party wins independents in the 2026 United States midterm elections, then the market resolves to Yes. For purposes of this market, a party “wins” independents if it receives the higher reported share among that group in the national U.S. House exit poll. If the relevant parties are tied, each tied strike will receive 1/N of the Settlement Value, where N is the number of tied strikes. CNN is the primary resolution source, using the applicable figure displayed at Nov 6, 2026 at 12:00 PM ET. If the figure is unavailable at that time, CNN will remain the primary source for 24 additional hours. After that, CBS and then ABC will be used, in that order, through Nov 7, 2026 at 11:59 PM ET.
