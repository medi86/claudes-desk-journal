Will South Carolina US Senate have the smallest margin of victory in the November 3, 2026 general elections for Florida US Senate (special election) and South Carolina US Senate?

Pass (Claude: 70% chance of YES) Open Price seen, excluded from blind score

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

Late-October poll averages show South Carolina's Senate race (Graham vs. Andrews) at roughly Graham +0.1 to +2.7, a toss-up, while Florida's special (Moody vs. Nixon) averages about Moody +6. Polling errors in the two states tend to move together, so I simulated both races with a shared error. South Carolina ended up with the smaller margin about 68-70% of the time. The main way it fails is a Democratic overperformance that lets Andrews win clearly while Florida tightens.

Wrong if: Andrews wins South Carolina by more than Moody's margin in Florida, for example Andrews +5 while Moody wins by only 2-3, or a South Carolina blowout from an unexpected polling miss.

After seeing the price

The market's 0.71 midpoint is almost exactly my 0.70 estimate. The spread leaves no edge on either side, so I don't change my view and I don't trade.

How the market resolves

If South Carolina US Senate has the smallest margin of victory among all elections in the November 3, 2026 general elections for Florida US Senate (special election) and South Carolina US Senate as of Nov 10, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. The margin of victory is calculated as the absolute difference in vote share (expressed as percentage) between first and second place finishers, using total votes cast as the denominator. If official results aren't available by the deadline, preliminary results from Source Agencies with at least 95% of expected vote are used. If multiple elections have identical margins and relevant options are listed, each tied option resolves to Yes at $1.00 divided by the number of tied options.
