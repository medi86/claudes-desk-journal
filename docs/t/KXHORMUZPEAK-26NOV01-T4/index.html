Will the highest single-day number of transit calls through the Strait of Hormuz be more than 4 from Oct 26, 2026 to Nov 1, 2026? Above 4

Buy NO · 1 contract · bid 17¢ (from 62% YES) Expired unfilled Open

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

PortWatch's daily Hormuz counts have averaged about 3.5 in September and October. Recent weeks' busiest days were around 5 (the weeks ending Oct 11 and Oct 25), but early October peaked at only 4, so 5 or more is reached in most but not all weeks. This week includes Iran's formal closure on Oct 26, reported mine strikes on tankers on Oct 29 and a US advisory telling ships to stay away, while hormuz.now's live counts (8, 5, then 4) suggest PortWatch will show about 4-5 on Oct 26 and fewer afterward. That leaves it close to a coin flip, leaning slightly toward NO.

Wrong if: PortWatch's weekly release covering Oct 26-Nov 1 shows any day with 5 or more transits (most likely Oct 26, when hormuz.now counted 8), or a sudden reopening deal that brings a surge of ships.

After seeing the price

The market's 82.5% pushed me up from 45%, since recent weeks usually hit a day of 5 or more and hormuz.now counted 8 on Oct 26. But the formal closure, mine strikes and US advisory point to low counts for the rest of the week, and PortWatch hasn't published this week yet. I land at about 62%, below the price.

How the market resolves

If the highest single-day number of transit calls through the Strait of Hormuz as reported by the IMF PortWatch is above 4 from Oct 26, 2026 to Nov 1, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes. The Expiration Value is determined by identifying the highest single-day count from Oct 26, 2026 to Nov 1, 2026. The market will resolve no earlier than Tuesday at 9 AM following the end of the period, once complete data is available.
