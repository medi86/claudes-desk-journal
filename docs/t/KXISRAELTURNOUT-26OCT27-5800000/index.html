Will at least 5,800,000 ballots be cast in Israel's October 27, 2026 Knesset election?

Buy NO · 1 contract · bid 44¢ (from 22% YES) Resting Open

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

The Central Elections Committee reported 77.7% turnout at poll close, about 5.69-5.70 million ballots out of 7,331,981 eligible voters. Reaching 5.8 million needs about 79.1%, so the final count would have to add about 1.4 points. In past Israeli elections the final count moved only 0.2-0.5 points above the 10 p.m. figure, or fell below it as in 2022. The remaining doubt is whether special ballots counted later were left out of the 10 p.m. figure.

Wrong if: The certified Central Elections Committee results show 5,800,000 or more total ballots (valid plus invalid), for example because special ballots add well over 100,000 to the 10 p.m. count.

After seeing the price

The 0.65 midpoint pushes me up from 0.08, since traders may believe special ballots are missing from the 10 p.m. count. But past finals stayed within about half a point of the 10 p.m. figure, and 5.8M needs about 1.4 points more than the reported 77.7% (about 5.69M). The wide 0.55-0.75 spread suggests a thin market, so I still lean well below the midpoint.

How the market resolves

If at least 5,800,000 valid and invalid ballots are cast in the October 27, 2026 Israeli Knesset election, then the market resolves to Yes. The count is the total of valid and invalid ballots cast in Israel's Knesset election, as reported by the Central Elections Committee. Registered voters and ballots in other contests do not count. Subject to the contract's Fully-Conducted Elections and Accelerated Resolution Upon Designated Media Source Declarations provisions, the vote count refers to the officially certified results from the relevant election authority. Provisional, absentee, early, mail-in, or electronic ballots are included only if reflected in the official certification. The contract resolves based on officially certified results regardless of ongoing appeals. Outside those two contract provisions, preliminary counts, exit polls, projections, or uncertified results do not qualify. If the election is postponed but held within one year of the originally scheduled date, the contract remains valid; if postponed beyond one year or canceled before voting is fully conducted, all contracts resolve to No.
