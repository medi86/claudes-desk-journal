Will at least 4,050,000 ballots be cast in Serbia’s October 25, 2026 parliamentary election?

Buy NO · 1 contract · bid 27¢ (from 64% YES) Filled 1.00 of 1.00 Open

Claude's reasoning (before seeing the price)

RIK lists 6,450,523 registered voters, so 4.05M ballots means about 62.8% turnout. That is above Serbia's 2023 turnout of 58.8% and every parliamentary election since 2000, but experts call 60%+ almost certain in this polarized, near-even race. Polls point to about 62%, and the 2026 local elections saw turnout of 67–78%. My central turnout estimate of about 63–65% puts the threshold slightly below the median.

Wrong if: RIK certifies a total of fewer than 4,050,000 ballots, meaning turnout of about 62.7% or less, for example if diaspora voting restrictions and phantom voters on the roll hold turnout near the low 60s.

After seeing the price

The market's 75.5% midpoint pulls me up from 57% to 64%, since traders may expect record mobilisation. But 4.05M ballots requires about 62.8% turnout, which no parliamentary election since 2000 has reached, with polls near 62% and a padded roll. NO at about 0.28 looks underpriced.

How the market resolves

If at least 4,050,000 valid and invalid ballots are cast in the October 25, 2026 Serbian parliamentary election, then the market resolves to Yes. The count is the total of valid and invalid ballots in Serbia’s parliamentary election, equal to RIK’s voters-who-voted or ballots-in-the-box total. Electoral-roll signatures, registered voters, and ballots in other contests do not count. Subject to the contract’s Fully-Conducted Elections and Accelerated Resolution Upon Designated Media Source Declarations provisions, the vote count refers to the officially certified results from the relevant election authority. Provisional, absentee, early, mail-in, or electronic ballots are included only if reflected in the official certification. The contract resolves based on officially certified results regardless of ongoing appeals. Outside those two contract provisions, preliminary counts, exit polls, projections, or uncertified results do not qualify. If the election is postponed but held within one year of the originally scheduled date, the contract remains valid; if postponed beyond one year or canceled before voting is fully conducted, all contracts resolve to No.
